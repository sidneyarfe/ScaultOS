# Chapter 3: Pricing — The Commodity Problem

## Core Idea
"Grow or Die" — a business that isn't growing faster than the market (roughly 9% baseline, 20-30% in hot markets) is dying by definition. There are exactly three levers for growth, and commoditization (competing on price because your product is indistinguishable from competitors') is what caps all three at once. A Grand Slam Offer fixes this by making price comparison impossible.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Growth Levers**: get more customers, increase their average purchase value, get them to buy more times — the only three ways a business grows.
  - When to use: as the master checklist any time revenue is flat — ask which of the three is under-optimized.
  - How: `New Clients/mo × Lifetime Value = max revenue`. Growth = sell more clients (with maintained margin) or make each client worth more (higher price per purchase, or more purchases).
- **Commoditized vs. Grand Slam money math**: a side-by-side model showing how a Grand Slam Offer multiplies response rate × conversion rate × price to compound into total revenue gain, not just add to it.
  - When to use: to model the expected ROI of redesigning an offer, not just marketing copy.
  - How: `Response Rate multiplier × Conversion multiplier × Price multiplier = total revenue multiplier`. In Hormozi's example: 2.5x response × 2.5x conversion × 4x price = 22.4x more cash collected for the same ad spend.

## Key Concepts
- **Gross Profit**: revenue minus the direct cost of servicing one additional customer (not net profit, which subtracts all expenses).
- **Lifetime Value (LTV)**: gross profit accrued over a customer's entire relationship — gross margin × average months retained (Hormozi also calls this LTGP, Lifetime Gross Profit, when emphasizing it's profit-based, not revenue-based).
- **Commoditized offer**: a product available from many places, purchased on price because it's perceived as equal to alternatives.
- **Differentiated / "category of one"**: an offer that removes direct comparison, so the prospect's decision becomes "buy this or nothing" rather than "buy this or the cheaper alternative."

## Mental Models
- **Sell in a vacuum**: design the offer so it cannot be benchmarked against any competitor — the prospect's only real choice is your offer vs. inaction, not your offer vs. a substitute.
- **Market efficiency drives commodities to "just enough" margin**: in a commoditized category, competition mechanically pushes price down until margins only cover survival — this isn't a moral failing of the business owner, it's what commoditization does structurally.

## Anti-patterns
- **Competing on being "competitively priced"**: chasing "competitive" pricing means a client who finds anything cheaper elsewhere will leave — margins erode to nothing and the business becomes a slave to its own market.
- **Assuming fulfillment quality alone differentiates**: Hormozi is explicit that the fulfillment work is often identical between a commodity offer and a Grand Slam Offer — the difference is entirely in how the offer is constructed and presented, not the underlying service.

## Worked Example
Old (commoditized) lead-gen agency offer: $1,000 down + $1,000/mo retainer. At 0.5-1x return on ad spend, the agency loses money acquiring the first 5 clients, breaks even around day 30, and only becomes profitable in month two — a slow, fragile, price-comparable offer.
New (Grand Slam) offer for the same agency: pay one time, no retainer, cover ad spend only, pay only for leads that show up, guaranteed 20 people in month one or the next month free, plus the entire industry playbook (scripts, sales coaching, tested price points) thrown in free. Result: 2.5x more people respond (better offer), 2.5x more of those convert (better offer), and the agency charges 4x more up front — a 22.4x multiplier on cash collected for the identical ad spend, because none of these levers move independently; they compound.

## Key Takeaways
1. Growth has exactly three levers (more customers, higher value per purchase, more purchases) — any stalled business is failing on at least one.
2. Commoditization isn't about product quality; it's about whether a prospect *can* compare you to alternatives — remove the ability to compare, and price stops being the deciding factor.
3. A Grand Slam Offer's ROI is multiplicative, not additive — small gains in response rate, conversion, and price compound into order-of-magnitude revenue differences for the same ad spend.
4. Applying a Grand Slam Offer to the wrong market fails regardless of offer quality — market selection (next chapter) is a prerequisite, not an afterthought.
