# Chapter 1: How We Got Here

## Core Idea
Hormozi's near-bankruptcy story (Christmas Eve 2016 — partner stole $45,700, he owed a $22,000 commission he didn't have) is the origin of the book: he launched six gyms simultaneously on a maxed $100K credit card, burning $3,300/day, using nothing but a Grand Slam Offer to survive. The skill of making offers is presented as the single lever that took him from $1,036 in the bank to $120M+ in sales in 24 months.

## Frameworks Introduced
(None yet — this chapter is the origin story that motivates the rest of the book.)

## Key Concepts
- **Grand Slam Offer**: the (not-yet-defined) offer methodology Hormozi credits with the entire turnaround — formally defined in Ch2.
- **Gym Launch**: Hormozi's business at the time — flying to gyms nationwide and filling them to capacity using his offer methodology.

## Mental Models
- **Betting big under asymmetric odds**: the Jeff Bezos quote that opens the book — in business, unlike a casino, skill shifts the odds in your favor, and a long-tailed outcome distribution means a single "grand slam" can outweigh many failures. Use this to justify bold, well-designed bets rather than incremental ones.
- **You only need one Grand Slam Offer**: Hormozi frames success as needing to "hit" one great offer, not many good ones, to change a business's trajectory permanently.

## Anti-patterns
- **Waiting until you feel ready**: Hormozi launched six gyms at once while functionally bankrupt, with no safety net — the book's implicit stance is that clarity on the offer matters more than capital or timing.

## Worked Example
The turnaround, month by month: starting balance $1,036 (post-crisis) → six gyms launched simultaneously on Dec 26, 2016, spending $3,300/day (~$412/working hour) on ads across airfare, hotels, rentals, and ad spend → January 2017 revenue: $100,117, just enough to cover the burn → by end of year: $1,500,000+/mo → 12 months later: $4,400,000/mo → 24 months after that: $120,000,000+ cumulative sales. The mechanism credited for the recovery is a single well-built offer applied at scale, not new capital, new hires, or a new market.

## Key Takeaways
1. A single well-constructed offer can be the deciding factor between bankruptcy and scale — the rest of the book is the mechanics of building one.
2. Betting boldly makes sense when the downside is capped (time/money you're already about to lose) and the upside is asymmetric (a "grand slam" pays for many strikeouts).
3. The book positions itself as a tool to return to repeatedly, not a one-time read — treat it as a working reference, not a narrative.
