# Cheatsheet — $100M Offers

## Decision Rules

- **Conversion is flat/declining** → check offer-fatigue order before rebuilding the offer: creative → copy → name → duration → discount → pricing. Never jump straight to restructuring monetization.
- **Tempted to discount to close a deal** → add a bonus or guarantee instead. Discounting teaches the prospect your price is negotiable; a bonus doesn't.
- **A "but what about X?" objection recurs across multiple prospects** → it's a missing offer component, not a client-education problem. Build the solution once, permanently.
- **Deciding market vs. offer vs. persuasion to fix first** → fix in that priority order (`Starving Crowd > Offer > Persuasion`). A bad market caps everything downstream regardless of offer quality.
- **First time launching a new offer** → over-deliver manually to prove it sells before optimizing fulfillment cost ("create flow, monetize flow, then add friction").
- **Considering a stronger guarantee** → run the guarantee math (net sales before/after) before rejecting it out of fear of refunds; the sales lift usually dwarfs the refund-rate increase.
- **High fulfillment cost per client** → skip unconditional guarantees; use conditional (action-gated) or implied (performance-based) guarantees instead.
- **A generic offer underperforms** → niche the avatar down one more level before assuming the offer itself is wrong; price can often 25-100x for the same core content.

## Guarantee Type Selection

| Situation | Guarantee type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Low ticket, low fulfillment cost, B2C | Unconditional | Max conversion lift; refund exposure per unit is small |
| High ticket, high fulfillment cost, or B2B | Conditional | Shifts accountability onto documented client actions |
| Exclusive/easy-to-copy IP | Anti-Guarantee | "All sales final" + credible vulnerability reason filters for commitment |
| Outcome is fully within your control (agency, ads) | Implied (performance/revshare) | Payment-on-results aligns incentives; downside is tracking/collection overhead |

## Scarcity/Urgency Mechanism Selection

| Business shape | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Physical product / limited drop | Limited Supply (never-again or seasonal release) |
| Ongoing service, want a waitlist | Total Business Cap |
| Ongoing service, steady intake | Growth Rate Cap (X/week) |
| Cohort-based delivery | Cohort Cap + Cohort-Based Rolling Urgency |
| Sell year-round, can't restrict service itself | Pricing/Bonus-Based Urgency (deadline on the discount, not the service) |
| Genuinely capacity-constrained | Honest Scarcity (publish real % to capacity) |

## Thresholds & Defaults

- Charge **up to 100x** cost to fulfill, not 2-3x — the ceiling is what's worth it to the customer, not a small multiple of your cost.
- Niching down increases price **25x-100x** for the same core content, most reliably below **$10M/yr** revenue.
- On a week-long launch, **50-60% of sales** land in the final **4 hours** — deadlines convert, don't fear them.
- Guarantee math breakeven: a guarantee is worth it unless the refund-rate increase (in percentage points) fully offsets the sales-conversion increase.
- Demand is fractal, not linear: roughly the top **20%** of prospects will pay **5x+** the price the rest will pay — consider skimming instead of uniform pricing.
- A real problem list for a offer typically runs **16 core problems × 2-4 sub-problems = 32-64 total** — a much shorter list signals under-development.
- M-A-G-I-C names: use **3-5 of 5** components — using all five usually makes the name too long.

## Tells & Smells

- **"We're pricing competitively"** → tell that the business is sliding toward commoditization; there's no strategic benefit to being second-cheapest.
- **One specific objection keeps killing sales** → tell that a bonus/guarantee is missing, not that the market or offer is fundamentally wrong.
- **An offer that sold out easily still under-converts next cycle** → tell that supply wasn't cut aggressively enough relative to demand, or the sell-out wasn't publicized.
- **A market that "should" work but doesn't, despite a good offer and good sales skill** → tell to check if the market itself is shrinking (the newspaper example) before touching anything else.
- **A new offer requires increasingly heroic personal effort to keep selling** → tell that fulfillment needs re-engineering (Sales-to-Fulfillment Continuum), not that the offer itself is wrong.
- **Repeatedly satisfying 100% of demand at launch** → tell that future launches will convert worse each time; pent-up, unsatisfied demand is what makes the next cycle work.
