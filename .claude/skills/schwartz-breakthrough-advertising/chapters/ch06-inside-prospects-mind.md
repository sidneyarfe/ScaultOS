# Chapter 6: Inside Your Prospect's Mind — What Makes People Read, Want, Believe

## Core Idea
Opens Part 2 (the Seven Techniques of Breakthrough Copy) by naming the three dimensions of the reader's mind that body copy must work on simultaneously: Desires, Identifications, and Beliefs. Every technique in Ch. 7–13 is a tool for expanding one or more of these three.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Dimensions of the Prospect's Mind**:
  1. **Desires** — wants, needs, cravings for *physical/material/sensual satisfaction*. Already exist; cannot be created, only sharpened, channeled, and given a goal. Tool: **Intensification** (Ch. 7).
  2. **Identifications** — the *roles* the prospect wants to play and the personality they want the product to project or acknowledge. Unspoken, often unconscious, expressed through symbols, not direct statements. Tool: **Identification** (Ch. 8).
  3. **Beliefs** — the prospect's existing map of what's true, how the world works, and what feels safe. Copy cannot fight or reverse beliefs; it must build a bridge from what's already accepted to the new claim. Tools: **Gradualization** (Ch. 9), **Redefinition** (Ch. 10), **Mechanization** (Ch. 11), **Concentration** (Ch. 12).
  - How to use: for any given ad, ask which of the three dimensions is the weakest link — is the reader under-desiring, unable to see themselves as the user, or simply unconvinced? Weight your body copy toward whichever dimension needs the most work.

## Key Concepts
- **Copy length is not a stylistic choice — it's a function of dimension gaps.** Ad length should be determined by three questions: (1) how much copy is needed to build desire for the product to its fullest strength, (2) how much is needed to make the reader comfortable/complimented enough to identify with owning it, (3) how much is needed to overcome existing skepticism and beliefs. Short ads answer these quickly for a hot, aware, unsophisticated market; long ads are required when any dimension has a long way to travel.
- **Absolute Conviction** is the fusion target: desire alone produces wanting without trust; belief alone produces intellectual agreement without wanting. Only desire *fused with* belief (and reinforced by identification) produces the buying decision.

## Mental Models
- Picture the three dimensions as three separate rivers feeding into one lake (the purchase decision). A copywriter who only intensifies desire while ignoring belief will produce readers who want the product and don't trust the claim — the classic "sounds too good to be true" failure mode.
- **Blending and Reinforcement**: in an actual ad, the three dimensions are never handled in strictly separate sections — they're woven together sentence by sentence (fully developed in Ch. 14's Interweaving technique) so that desire-image, identification-cue, and belief-signal often appear inside the very same sentence.

## Worked Example
A single product claim run through all three lenses:
- **Desire-only version**: "This spark plug gives you 31 more horsepower." (states the want, nothing more)
- **+ Identification**: place the product in a sports-car garage scene with a precise, virile mechanic — now the reader who wants to *see himself* as a knowledgeable enthusiast is pulled in, not just someone who wants more horsepower.
- **+ Belief**: add the mechanism explanation (larger spark = bigger explosion = more power, explained step by step) so the horsepower claim isn't just wanted, it's *believed*.

Only the third version, with all three dimensions engaged, produces Absolute Conviction rather than mere interest.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose which of the three dimensions (Desire / Identification / Belief) is weakest for your specific reader before deciding how long or what shape the copy needs to be.
2. Desire without belief produces "too good to be true" rejection; belief without desire produces indifferent agreement — you need both.
3. Ad length is not a style preference; it's the sum of how far each of the three dimensions has to travel from where the reader starts.
4. All seven techniques that follow (Ch. 7–13) are specialized tools for expanding one of these three dimensions — use this chapter as the index.
