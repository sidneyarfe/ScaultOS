# Chapter 15: Epilogue — A Copy Writer's Library

## Core Idea
Schwartz closes with a short, opinionated reading list — the books he considers essential to keep re-reading, not just read once. This chapter is a reference, not a technique.

## Key Concepts (recommended library, as named by Schwartz)
- **Julian Watkins, *The 100 Greatest Advertisements*** (Moore Publishing, 1949) — anthology, "indispensable."
- **Claude Hopkins, *My Life in Advertising*** (Harper, 1927) — the founding text of "reason-why" copy; worth tracking down original Hopkins ads too.
- **John E. Kennedy, *Intensive Advertising*** — a short pamphlet Schwartz calls "the most profitable evening's reading you've ever done."
- **The Lasker Story** (a series of talks by Albert Lasker, captured by Printers Ink) — insider history from the Lord & Thomas era.
- **Robert Collier, *The Robert Collier Letter Book*** (Prentice-Hall) — "a gold mine"; source of the price-reduction mechanization pattern used in Ch. 10.
- **David Ogilvy, *Confessions of an Advertising Man*** — "charming and wonderfully profitable."
- **Rosser Reeves, *Reality in Advertising*** (Knopf) — short, dense; Schwartz says it needs several re-reads.
- **Milton Biow, *Butting In*** — strong first half.
- **Walter S. Campbell, *Writing Non-Fiction*** (The Writer, Inc., 1961) — the direct source of Ch. 14's Interweaving technique; Schwartz calls it a book "every copy writer should read... you can probably master in a weekend."
- Plus: Caples, Young, Glim, and Bedell on craft; Dichter and Martineau on motivation research; Baker's *Visual Persuasion* on layout; and broader social-science reading (Mills, Riesman, Fromm, Kardiner) for understanding the culture the copy has to speak into.

## Mental Model
Schwartz's closing instruction is a discipline, not a suggestion: "Keep reading. Keep analyzing. Keep writing." He frames continuous close-reading of other advertisers' work — not just theory books — as an ongoing part of the job, alongside actually writing copy.
