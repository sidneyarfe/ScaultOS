# Chapter 2: Your Prospect's State of Awareness

## Core Idea
Your headline's only job is to get the second sentence read — it never has to sell alone. What it *must* do is start exactly where your reader's mind already is. Match the headline to the prospect's awareness stage, or the headline is either wasted (too basic) or unbelievable (too advanced).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 5 Stages of Awareness** (Schwartz's foundational framework — still the industry's default mental model for headline strategy):
  1. **Most Aware** — knows the product, what it does, wants it, just hasn't bought. Headline = name + price/bargain. No creativity required, no persuasion needed.
  2. **Product-Aware** — knows the product exists and roughly what it does but isn't sold yet. Headline has one of 7 jobs: reinforce desire (via association/example/sensory sharpening/illustration), sharpen the image of *how* it satisfies, extend *where/when* it satisfies, introduce new proof, announce a new mechanism, announce removal of an old limitation, or completely reposition the product (this last one bleeds into Ch. 3's sophistication problem).
  3. **Solution-Aware** ("New Products") — prospect recognizes the desire but doesn't know *your product* satisfies it. Three-step headline: name the desire/solution, prove it's achievable, show your product contains the mechanism.
  4. **Problem-Aware** — has a *need*, not yet a desire, and doesn't connect it to your product. Same three-step structure, but starting from naming the problem, not the desire.
  5. **Completely Unaware** — doesn't know the need exists, won't admit it, or the desire is too amorphous/taboo to state directly. This is the hardest and most valuable stage to unlock.
  - How to use: before writing a headline, diagnose which of the 5 stages your *specific* market segment sits in today — not where you wish they were.

## Key Concepts
- **Headline's real job**: not to sell — to stop the reader and force the second sentence to be read. Treating the headline as though it must close the sale wastes 80-90% of your ad's real estate.
- **Believability = readiness to accept**: a headline can only work if the prospect already has, or the copy has built, the groundwork to accept it. A headline that jumps stages fails even if the claim is true.

## Mental Models
- Think of awareness stages as **walls**, not a gradient — "each of these stages is separated from the others by a psychological wall. On one side is indifference; on the other, intense interest." A headline tuned for stage 1 (bargain price) will bore a stage-3 prospect and mystify a stage-5 prospect. A headline tuned for stage 5 (pure identification, no product mention) will feel evasive to a stage-1 prospect who already wants to buy.
- **The Fifth Stage rule of exclusion**: when the market is completely unaware, you may NOT mention price, product name, or a direct statement of the desire/problem/function. All three fail — price means nothing to someone with no want yet; the name means nothing (or actively hurts, if associated with something unfashionable); a direct claim will not be believed because the product hasn't earned the right to make it yet. What's left is *the market itself* — you write an **identification headline** that names the reader to themselves, without selling anything explicitly.

## Worked Example
Comparing headlines across the awareness ladder for the same underlying desire (weight loss):
- **Stage 1 (Most Aware)**: "Bargain-Brand Diet Pills — Now $9.98, Was $14.98" (price is the whole story)
- **Stage 2 (Product-Aware)**: "First Wonder Drug for Reducing!" (new mechanism claim to re-earn attention)
- **Stage 3 (Solution-Aware)**: "Now! Lose Ugly Fat!" (names the desire directly — market has never heard the claim before)
- **Stage 5 (Unaware/identification)**: Schwartz's Postum coffee-substitute case — instead of any diet/health claim, the ad opened with "WHY MEN CRACK…", an identification headline about overwork and stimulant dependency that *never mentions the product by name in the headline* and only reveals Postum as the answer three paragraphs later, after the reader has already recognized themselves in the copy.

The lesson: the exact same underlying desire needs four structurally different headlines depending on what the market already believes.

## Key Takeaways
1. A headline never has to do the full selling job — only get the next sentence read.
2. Diagnose the market's real awareness stage before writing; don't assume stage 1 or stage 5 by default.
3. At the Most Aware stage, price/bargain does all the work — save your creative energy elsewhere.
4. At the Completely Unaware stage, ban price, product name, and direct claims from the headline — lead with an identification statement instead.
5. A headline that worked yesterday can fail today purely because the market has *moved* to a new awareness stage — this is not a copy problem, it's a market-tracking problem.

## Connects To
- **Ch 1**: awareness stage tells you *which* mass desire to lead with and how explicitly to state it.
- **Ch 3**: market sophistication (how many competitors have already used this claim) compounds with awareness stage — a market can be Product-Aware *and* Third-Stage sophisticated at once, which changes what the headline needs to say.
- **Ch 9 (Gradualization)**: the identification-headline technique for unaware markets is a direct application of building belief before stating the claim.
