# Chapter 11: The Fifth Technique — Mechanization

## Core Idea
Every claim triggers a silent reader question: "How does it work?" Mechanization is the verbal, logical proof — built from wording alone, no outside evidence required — that the product actually delivers what it promises. Classic "reason-why" copy.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three Stages of Mechanism Depth** (match depth to what the market already accepts — Ch. 2/3 diagnosis applies directly):
  1. **Name it** — if the market already understands and accepts the mechanism from prior advertising, just name it in bold type and move on ("Electronic Light Setter... Push-Button Zomar Lens..."). Over-explaining a familiar mechanism wastes space and bores an already-convinced reader.
  2. **Describe it** — if the mechanism is unfamiliar, walk through Promise → Reason-Why in sequence: state the benefit, then explain *why* it's true, in selling language (never dry/scientific), reinforcing the same promise while proving it ("Dirt floats off — stains go. The secret is simply Rinso — a mild, granulated soap that gives rich, lasting suds...").
  3. **Feature it** — when the market is sophisticated (Ch. 3, stage 3+) and every competitor is making the same promise, the mechanism itself becomes strong enough to *carry the headline* ("FLOATS FAT RIGHT OUT OF YOUR BODY!", "RUN YOUR CAR WITHOUT SPARK PLUGS!"). This is the direct bridge into Ch. 3's stage-3 sophistication strategy.
- **The one hard rule of mechanization**: even mechanism/proof copy must still *sell* — never let it go dry or purely technical. Every mechanism sentence should reinforce the same desire it's proving, not just state facts. Compare the flat version ("suds lift dirt") to the sold version ("Dirt floats off — stains go") — same content, very different pull.
- **Price-cut mechanization** (Robert Collier pattern, cross-referenced from Ch. 10): a discount needs its own mechanism too — a believable *reason* the price is temporarily low (overstock, factory S.O.S., special buy) makes the bargain credible instead of suspicious.

## Key Concepts
- **Reason-why copy**: the term of art (going back to Claude Hopkins) for any copy segment whose job is to explain the *how* behind a claim, in language that still sells rather than merely informs.
- **Verbal proof vs. documented proof**: Mechanization proves through logical structure and vivid explanation alone — no third-party statistics or testimonials required (those belong to Verification, Ch. 14). A well-built mechanism explanation can feel as convincing as external evidence.

## Mental Models
- Ask, for every claim you write: "if the reader silently asked 'how?' right now, does my very next sentence answer it — in a way that keeps selling?" If not, insert a Stage 2 mechanism beat before moving on.
- When market sophistication has exhausted the promise itself (Ch. 3, stage 3), the mechanism *is* the fresh idea — treat "what's the new *how*" as the primary creative question, not "what's the new benefit."

## Worked Example
Schwartz's fire-injector spark-plug ad runs full Stage-2 mechanization, sentence by sentence, using constant Bad→Good pairing (previewing Ch. 12's Concentration): "A spark plug jumps a spark of electricity across an air gap. This is the most wasteful and power-consuming way to get electricity... A fire injector fires on the surface of an electrical conductor. This is the most efficient way to get a big, powerful spark into your cylinder." Each mechanism sentence both *explains* (this is how it works) and *sells* (and that's why it's better) in the same breath — never one without the other.

## Key Takeaways
1. Match mechanism depth to market awareness/sophistication: name it if already accepted, describe it if unfamiliar, feature it in the headline if the promise alone is exhausted.
2. Never let mechanism copy go purely factual — every "how" sentence should also reinforce the underlying desire.
3. A discount needs its own believable "why now, why this low" mechanism — state the reason before the number.
4. When a whole market's claims are exhausted (Ch. 3, stage 3+), a genuinely new mechanism is usually the fastest way back into growth — faster than trying to escalate the promise again.
