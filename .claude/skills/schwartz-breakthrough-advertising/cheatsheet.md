# Cheatsheet — Breakthrough Advertising Decision Guide

## Decision 1: What should the headline say?
Diagnose **awareness stage** first — this decides what the headline is *allowed* to mention.

| Awareness Stage | Headline may include | Headline must NOT include |
|---|---|---|
| Most Aware | Product name + price/bargain | Nothing else needed |
| Product-Aware | Sharpened/extended claim about known product | New unrelated desire |
| Solution-Aware | The desire, stated directly, + hint the product delivers it | Assuming they know your brand solves it |
| Problem-Aware | The problem/need, crystallized into words | The product name (usually premature) |
| Completely Unaware | An identification statement about the reader | Price, product name, direct claim of any kind |

**Rule of thumb**: if unsure between two adjacent stages, write for the *less* aware one — it's safer to slightly over-explain than to lose an unprepared reader.

## Decision 2: How should the claim be worded?
Diagnose **market sophistication** — this decides claim size and whether mechanism belongs in the headline.

| Sophistication Stage | What goes in the headline |
|---|---|
| 1st (first in market) | The bare claim, simply stated. No escalation. |
| 2nd | The claim, enlarged/escalated past what's been said before. |
| 3rd | A NEW MECHANISM name — claim exhausted, mechanism is now the news. |
| 4th | The mechanism, elaborated/improved past competitors' version of it. |
| 5th (exhausted) | No claim, no mechanism — pure identification headline. |

**Tell**: if 3+ competitors are already making your exact claim with similar wording, you're at stage 3+ — stop trying to out-promise them and find/feature a mechanism instead.

## Decision 3: Is this claim ready to be stated, or does it need preparation first?
**Rule**: never open with your strongest, most surprising claim raw.
- If the claim would make a skeptical reader think "yeah, right" → build 2-4 sentences of accepted micro-statements first (inclusion question, detailed symptom description, logic-language) before stating it.
- If the claim is mild/expected → state it directly, no preparation needed.
- **Default**: when in doubt, move the claim *later* in the copy rather than making it *louder* — position beats volume for believability.

## Decision 4: My product has an obvious weakness/objection. Which fix?

| The objection sounds like... | Use | Core move |
|---|---|---|
| "Too complicated / hard to learn" | Simplification | Name the smallest real unit of effort (not "it's easy" — the actual number/step) |
| "Not worth caring about" | Escalation | Widen the product's role in the reader's whole life |
| "Too expensive" | Price Reduction | State a credible higher anchor (with a reason) BEFORE the real price |

## Decision 5: Should I attack the competition?

- **Dominate the category?** → Stay silent. Attacking gives a smaller rival free attention.
- **Underdog, need to break existing loyalty?** → Use Concentration, but ONLY if every named weakness is immediately paired with your fix in the same breath.
- **Offer is genuinely different, nothing comparable exists?** → Skip Concentration; naming a non-comparable rival wastes space.
- **Test before publishing**: delete your product's name from the attack paragraph. Does it still read as useful information, or does it collapse into pure disparagement? If it collapses, add the fix.

## Decision 6: How much "how it works" explanation does this need?

| Market already... | Mechanization depth |
|---|---|
| Believes/understands the mechanism from other ads | Name it only (bold type, move on) |
| Is unfamiliar with the mechanism | Describe it — Promise, then Reason-Why, in selling language |
| Has heard this exact promise from every competitor | Feature it — put the mechanism in the headline itself |

## Decision 7: What's this product's default self-image, and can I use it?

1. What does the market already believe about this product's character? (Write it down — this is the **primary image**, favorable or not.)
2. Is it favorable? → **Intensify**: stack 2-3 more symbols reinforcing the SAME trait.
3. Is it neutral/unfavorable? → **Subordinate + bridge**: keep the primary image small but present, then build a bigger, believable target image around it. Never contradict it outright.
4. Before shipping any identification image, run the **believability-bridge test**: (a) does the market already believe the product has this trait? (b) can the reader connect it to their own life? Both "no" → insert a bridge image first.

## Fast reference: the 3 dimensions and their toolkits

| Dimension | What it is | Primary technique(s) |
|---|---|---|
| **Desire** | Physical/material/sensual want | Intensification (Ch 7) |
| **Identification** | Self-image / role the product confers | Identification (Ch 8) |
| **Belief** | Existing map of what's true/safe | Gradualization, Redefinition, Mechanization, Concentration (Ch 9-12) |

**Diagnostic**: reread your draft and ask which dimension is weakest. Under-desiring reader → add Intensification. Reader can't picture themselves using it → add Identification. Reader doesn't trust the claim → add Gradualization/Mechanization/Verification.

## Camouflage quick picks

- **Sophisticated/skeptical audience** → Understatement (short sentences, few adjectives, self-kidding tone).
- **Need instant credibility on a doubted claim** → Deadly Sincerity (admit one real, minor flaw before the claim).
- **Placing in a specific trusted publication** → Format-match its actual headline case, subhead style, and column conventions — test: would it pass as their own editorial content at a glance?

## Momentum/Mood self-check (before shipping any ad)

- [ ] Does every paragraph offer a genuinely NEW angle on the same one desire (not a repeat)?
- [ ] Is there a transition phrase or incomplete-statement hook at every natural drop-off point?
- [ ] Read the copy aloud — does the rhythm (short/staccato vs. long/flowing) match the intended emotion?
- [ ] Delete all image-sharpener/emotion-definer words — do the core claims still hold up? (If not, the underlying claim is too thin, not just under-decorated.)
- [ ] Is there exactly ONE dominant desire in the headline — not two competing promises?
