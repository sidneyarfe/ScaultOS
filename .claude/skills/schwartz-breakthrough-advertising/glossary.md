# Glossary — Breakthrough Advertising (Eugene Schwartz)

**Absolute Conviction** — the fusion target of all copy: desire fused with belief, reinforced by identification, that produces the buying decision (Ch 6).

**Achievement Roles** — status/class/position roles created by society (Executive, Homeowner) that a buyer displays through the products they own, because achievement itself is invisible without a physical marker (Ch 8).

**Amplification Effect** — the outsized return ($50-$100 per $1) advertising gets by directing an already-existing mass desire, versus trying to create desire from scratch, which only returns $1 per $1 like education (Ch 1).

**Belief** — the third dimension of the prospect's mind: their existing map of what's true and safe. Cannot be reversed by copy, only bridged (Ch 6, Ch 9).

**Believability-Bridge** — an already-accepted image used as a hook to connect the reader's current belief to a new, more desirable target claim (Ch 8, Ch 9).

**Camouflage** — the seventh technique: borrowing believability from the trusted medium's format, phraseology, and mood (Ch 13).

**Catalog Technique** — a rapid-fire closing list of benefits/applications, used horizontally (many applications) or vertically (many depths of one desire), after precision copy, not instead of it (Ch 7).

**Central Campaign Idea** — the fixed slogan or visual anchor of a repeated/national campaign, around which supporting execution rotates for freshness (Ch 7).

**Character Roles** — adjective-based identity traits (chic, brilliant, well-read) a buyer wants to express through a product, usually unstated and unconscious (Ch 8).

**Concentration** — the sixth technique: direct attack on competitors or alternative solutions, always paired with your product's fix for the named weakness (Ch 12).

**Contingency Structures** — belief-building sentence forms like "If... then..." used to make a claim feel logically derived (Ch 9).

**Creative Gamble** — withholding the product name/brand from a headline, betting the body copy alone will close the sale in exchange for a stronger curiosity pull (Ch 7).

**Deadly Sincerity** — admitting a real flaw or limitation before making a positive claim, so the reader trusts the claim that follows because the ad proved willing to be honest (Ch 13).

**Doctrina/Doctrine of No Formula Works Twice** — Schwartz's core creative-methodology claim: every winning formula is unique to its product-market-timing moment and cannot be reliably reused (Ch 3, Ch 5).

**Echo Ads** — headlines copied word-for-word from another product's proven formula, without matching product/market/timing — usually fail (Ch 4, Ch 5).

**Emotion-Definers** — words that tell the reader invisibly how to feel about an image (precious, blinded, greets) without calling attention to themselves (Ch 14).

**Fifth Stage of Awareness (Completely Unaware)** — market not aware of the need/desire, won't admit it, or it's too amorphous/taboo to state; headline must ban price, product name, and direct claims (Ch 2).

**Fifth Stage of Sophistication (Exhausted)** — market numb to every claim in a category; requires either a genuinely new mechanism or a pure identification headline (Ch 3).

**Functional Product** — what the product *does* for the buyer; this, not the physical product, is what's actually sold (Ch 1).

**Goal Conclusion** — the actual sales claim the copy is building toward through a stream of accepted micro-statements (Ch 9).

**Gradualization** — the third technique: architecting copy so each claim lands on a foundation the reader has already agreed to, rather than stating raw claims cold (Ch 9).

**Identification** — the second technique: building a saleable personality/role into the product so buying it also buys a piece of self-image (Ch 8).

**Identification Headline** — a headline that names the reader to themselves without mentioning product, price, or a direct claim; used for Fifth-Stage-unaware markets and Fifth-Stage-sophisticated markets (Ch 2, Ch 3).

**Inclusion Question** — an opening question the reader answers "yes" to, planting them in the "yes" group before the pitch begins (Ch 9).

**Intensification** — the first technique: building desire by presenting continually fresh angles on the same underlying promise (Ch 7).

**Interweaving** — packing promise + image + logic into the same sentence rather than sequential sentences, for maximum density (Ch 14).

**Language of Logic** — words like "therefore" and "the reason is" that transfer a feeling of inevitability onto a claim regardless of its actual proof (Ch 9).

**Mass Desire** — "the public spread of a private want"; the pre-existing force copy channels but never creates (Ch 1).

**Mechanism** — the specific process, ingredient, or method that makes a claim credible; the "how" behind a "what" (Ch 3, Ch 11).

**Mechanization** — the fifth technique: verbal, logical proof (no outside evidence needed) that the product delivers what it promises, in three depths: name it, describe it, feature it (Ch 11).

**Momentum** — devices (transition phrases, incomplete statements) that physically pull the reader forward through the copy (Ch 14).

**Mood** — word- and rhythm-choice as an emotional channel operating beneath literal content, via image-sharpeners and emotion-definers (Ch 14).

**Most Aware** — the first awareness stage: prospect knows the product, wants it, hasn't bought yet; headline needs only name + price (Ch 2).

**Physical Product** — the raw material (steel, glass, paper) of a product; never sells on its own, only justifies price and documents believability (Ch 1).

**Primary Image** — the existing, already-believable image a product carries in the prospect's mind before any ad is written; must be built on, never contradicted (Ch 8).

**Problem-Aware** — awareness stage where the prospect has a need but doesn't yet connect it to the product (Ch 2).

**Product-Aware** — awareness stage where the prospect knows the product exists but isn't sold yet (Ch 2).

**Reason-Why Copy** — Claude Hopkins-derived term for copy that explains the *how* behind a claim in selling language (Ch 11).

**Redefinition** — the fourth technique: reframing a product's built-in handicap (too hard / not important / too expensive) via Simplification, Escalation, or Price Reduction (Ch 10).

**Reinforcement** — the non-mathematical stacking effect where two well-juxtaposed claims produce more impact than their sum (Ch 14).

**Sensitivity** — the discipline of reading your own draft as the reader would, to detect when a copy-block should shift to a new type of material (Ch 14).

**Solution-Aware** — awareness stage where the prospect recognizes the desire but doesn't know this specific product satisfies it (Ch 2).

**Sophistication (Market Sophistication)** — how many competing products/claims the market has already been exposed to; 5 stages from first-in-market to exhausted (Ch 3).

**Splinter Markets** — deliberately targeting a sub-segment of a market to avoid direct competition with larger rivals, then using proven results there to invade the majority market later (Ch 5).

**State of Awareness** — how much the prospect already knows about the specific product and its ability to satisfy their desire; 5 stages (Ch 2).

**Stream of Acceptances** — a chain of small, logically-connected accepted statements built to carry the reader to the goal conclusion (Ch 9).

**Syllogistic Thinking** — explicit if-A-then-B logical chains used to make a claim feel derived rather than merely asserted (Ch 9).

**Turn, The** — the delicate transition point in Redefinition copy where the reframed vocabulary is established before the feared/scary term is finally used (Ch 10).

**Understatement** — a Camouflage mood technique: short sentences, few adjectives, self-deprecating whimsy, escaping the "hard-sell" reader-skepticism reflex (Ch 13).

**Verbalization** — the craft of wording a fixed strategic claim to maximize impact, distinct from and applied after choosing the claim's content (Ch 4).

**Verification** — placing documentation/proof exactly where the reader's mind is already demanding it (Ch 14).
