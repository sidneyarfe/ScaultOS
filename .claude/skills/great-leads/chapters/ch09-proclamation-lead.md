# Chapter 9: The Proclamation Lead

## Core Idea
The Proclamation Lead opens with a single, bold, startling declarative statement — a fact, opinion, or prediction — stated with total confidence, before any product mention. It is the archetype the authors coined specifically for this book (no prior copywriting literature had isolated it), built to jar a Completely Unaware or highly skeptical reader out of indifference through sheer audacity, then prove the statement true before circling back to the sale.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Proclamation Lead's four rules**:
  1. **Make the proclamation bold, not reasonable** — sensible, hedged statements don't stop anyone; the lead must startle to the point of near-disbelief.
  2. **Make (or imply) a promise** — even an indirect proclamation like "Read This Or Die" inverts into an implied promise ("if I read this, I won't die").
  3. **The subject must be relevant** — boldness without relevance to something the reader already cares about is just noise, not persuasion.
  4. **Return to the proclamation at the close** — echo the opening statement in the guarantee/premium language so the whole promotion feels unified (Rule of One at the structural level).
  - When to use: Completely Unaware or heavily-skeptical audiences, financial/political/health/environmental topics where a prediction or shocking fact naturally exists, situations calling for maximum pattern-interrupt.
  - How: find (through research, not invention) a genuinely remarkable, provable fact or defensible prediction; state it as a flat, confident assertion in the headline; spend the following copy proving it true before pivoting to product.
  - Why it works: ordinary, hedged claims are too easy to skim past — extraordinary, confidently-stated ones force the rational brain to pause and check ("is that really true?"), and that pause is the emotional hook.
- **The prediction sub-type**: a special form of Proclamation tied to a specific, often dated, future event ("Three Shocking Events will wipe out millions of American investors by Dec. 31, 2006") — linking a prediction to a concrete date measurably increases believability over an open-ended forecast.

## Key Concepts
- **Emotionally-compelling statement**: the authors' working definition for what a Proclamation Lead must be — considered and rejected alternate names included "declarations" and "pronouncements" before settling on "proclamations" for its connotation of energy.
- **Newspaper-story quality**: good Proclamation Leads read like tabloid or news headlines rather than advertising copy — the reader's rational brain may sense it's an ad, but the emotional brain suspends disbelief the way it does for a compelling news story.
- **Great Proclamation Leads are found in research, not written from imagination** — like Secret Leads, the "amazing fact" must be discovered (reams of documents, medical reports, financial charts) before the one-line proclamation can be distilled from it.

## Mental Models
- **The Proclamation is primarily indirect, but structurally different from a Secret Lead**: a Secret Lead withholds an answer; a Proclamation Lead states a shocking claim outright and then proves it — curiosity in a Secret Lead is "what is this?"; curiosity in a Proclamation Lead is "can that possibly be true?"
- **After the hook lands, other lead-type techniques can layer in** — a Proclamation opening frequently transitions into secret-style teasing (multiple withheld "recommendations"), story elements (named real people as proof), and only later an explicit Invitation-style Offer Lead — Proclamation is the entry point, not the whole structure.

## Worked Example
**"Read This Or Die"** (real health report-a-log cover): title alone is a near-Proclamation; the sub-head makes the actual proclamation explicit — "Today you have a 95% chance of eventually dying from a disease or condition for which there is already a known cure somewhere on the planet." The copy inside repeats and reinforces the claim ("after 6,000 years of recorded human history, we are finally able to issue this statement: the worldwide medical community has found solutions for all of the primary diseases and disorders of our time") before pivoting into an Invitation offer for a 150,000-member health journal.

**Bob Bly's PDR mailer** (B2B, real Physicians' Desk Reference promo): "WARNING: YOUR 1995 PDR IS OBSOLETE!" — a proclamation aimed at an audience (doctors) who are highly Product-Aware, proving Proclamation Leads aren't reserved only for Unaware audiences. Bly reinforces the same proclamation with an included sticker reading "WARNING: This edition of the PDR is out-of-date. Do not use this book for prescribing Information!" — a physical artifact that "ratchets up" the same core claim.

**Lee Euler's *The Plague of the Black Debt***: opens with a single massive prediction (a coming depression caused by debt) delivered through metaphor rather than abstract economics — comparing a stock-market crash to a literal plague made an otherwise-dry topic viscerally alarming. This mailed over 7 million times and generated 170,000+ orders; the "bookalog" format it introduced became an industry standard.

## Key Takeaways
1. The proclamation must be found through real research (a genuinely remarkable fact/statistic/study), not manufactured — readers can sense manufactured shock.
2. Bold beats reasonable — hedged, softened claims fail at this lead type's one job, which is to stop the reader cold.
3. Always link the proclamation to something the reader specifically cares about — audacity without relevance is just noise.
4. Predictions gain credibility from specific dates and named authorities far more than from open-ended forecasting language.
5. Close the loop — return to the original proclamation's language in the guarantee/premium so the whole piece reads as one unified idea (Rule of One).

## Connects To
- **Ch2 (Rule of One)**: the proclamation itself must be the single unifying idea the rest of the copy serves.
- **Ch3 (Awareness Scale)**: primarily for Completely Unaware audiences, but the PDR example proves it can also work on highly aware B2B audiences when the shock value is high enough.
- **Ch8 (Secret Lead)**: frequently blended — a Proclamation opens, then a Secret-style tease of "how to protect yourself" follows.
- **Ch10 (Story Lead)**: the other primary tool for Completely Unaware audiences, using narrative instead of assertion.
