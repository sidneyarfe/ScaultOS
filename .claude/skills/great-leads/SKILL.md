## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The central claim**: ~80% of a promotion's emotional impact comes from the first 20% of the copy — the "lead" (opening 100-600 words). Everything in this book is a system for engineering that opening deliberately instead of hoping to get lucky.

**The Rule of One** (the meta-rule underneath all six lead types): every strong lead is built around exactly ONE idea, ONE core emotion, ONE supporting story/fact, and points to ONE action. Never a "tossed salad" of several benefits hedged together — a narrow, deeply-developed idea beats a dozen shallow ones. You may combine techniques (a story that also contains a secret) but only in service of one unifying idea.

**Gene Schwartz's Five Levels of Customer Awareness** (the first diagnostic question — "what does my customer already know?"): Most Aware (knows the product, just needs the deal) → Product-Aware (knows it, isn't sure it's right for them) → Solution-Aware (wants the outcome, doesn't know you deliver it) → Problem-Aware (feels the pain, doesn't know a solution exists) → Completely Unaware (no frame of reference at all). This placement determines which lead type will actually work.

**The Directness Spectrum**: awareness level maps directly onto how direct vs. indirect the lead should be. More aware → more direct (Offer, Promise Leads state the deal/claim immediately). Less aware or more skeptical → more indirect (Secret, Proclamation, Story Leads delay or omit the pitch to build emotional connection first, bypassing "I don't want to be sold" resistance).

**The Six Lead Archetypes**:
1. **Offer Lead** — states the deal/price/guarantee immediately. Best: Most Aware. Formula: lead with the deal's most compelling detail → underscore its benefit → elaborate → always include a "reason why."
2. **Promise Lead** — opens with the product's single biggest claim. Best: Product-Aware/"on the fence." Find the promise via Rosser Reeves' USP test: true, original, aimed at a desire the prospect already has.
3. **Problem-Solution Lead** — validates the reader's core emotional worry before pivoting to the fix. Best: Problem-Aware/Solution-Aware. Find the "core emotion" underneath the surface symptom, not the symptom itself.
4. **Secret Lead** — names and teases withheld proprietary knowledge; never discloses it in the lead itself. Works across categories. Escalate specific clues, not the answer.
5. **Proclamation Lead** — a single, bold, confidently-stated fact/prediction, proven true before the pivot to product. Best: Completely Unaware or heavily skeptical. Must be found through real research, not invented.
6. **Story Lead** — opens with narrative (ordinary hero → doubted → proven right) instead of a claim. Arguably the most powerful archetype because it bypasses sales resistance entirely. Can be direct or indirect. Must keep the "Golden Thread" taut back to the product, or the story entertains without selling.

**Neologizing / transubstantiation**: inventing a new name for a mundane concept, or wrapping it in bigger historical/mythic framing, to make it feel proprietary or urgent (e.g., dividend stocks renamed "The Chaffee Royalty Program"). Powerful for Secret and Proclamation Leads — but the reveal must still be true and deliverable.

**Re-Audience, Re-Lead**: when a proven promotion underperforms on a new/wider list, don't rewrite the offer first — re-run the Awareness diagnostic on the new audience, then re-select the lead archetype. Real case studies in this book show 3x+ gains from this alone, same product, same deal.
